# API Dependency Sentinel — Campaign Execution Queue

**Status:** Prepared for founder review. No prospect or investor has been contacted through this queue.

## Operating Principle

The campaign is designed to collect evidence, not impressions. Every prospective message must be individually approved immediately before sending, use a verified public business or community-appropriate route, and accurately describe the product’s current read-only and human-review scope.

| Campaign lane | Objective | Current asset | Approval gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design partners | Find the repeated integration-maintenance problem and convert useful Risk Maps into paid monitoring pilots. | Ten reviewed drafts and verified organization-level routes. | Founder approves recipient, channel, personalization, and final copy one message at a time. |
| Product proof | Convert an accepted source-to-code finding into a permissioned or anonymized evidence walkthrough. | Live monitor, GitHub connection, provenance model, and demo script. | Customer permission before naming, quoting, or showing any repository-derived information. |
| Investor research | Build a small, high-fit investor pipeline around technical infrastructure and early enterprise software. | YC application material, investor thesis, and public firm research. | Founder approves every application submission or investor message. |

### Discovery Call Scorecard

| Question | Evidence to capture | Product decision it informs |
|---|---|---|
| What was the last provider change that created unexpected work? | Date, provider, affected system, team involved. | Whether the pain is recurring and urgent. |
| How was the relevant code found and assigned? | Tools, manual steps, elapsed time, ownership ambiguity. | Whether matching and ownership routing are valuable. |
| Which false positives would make this unusable? | Examples of irrelevant change classes or code evidence. | Provider filters, confidence thresholds, and workflow controls. |
| What artifact would be most useful? | Issue, test plan, pull request comment, dashboard, Slack alert. | Product integration priority. |
| What would a useful pilot be worth? | Budget range, buyer, approval path, timeline. | Pricing and conversion design. |

## 30-Day Execution Cadence

| Period | Sales activity | Product activity | Investor activity | Decision checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1–7 | Obtain final approval and send a maximum of five individual design-partner messages. | Complete live real-revision proof if an eligible match arrives. | Finalize the truthful YC application draft. | Stop and revise the message if prospects do not understand the problem. |
| Days 8–14 | Hold discovery calls and score them against the call scorecard. | Instrument accepted/rejected/uncertain findings and time-to-triage. | Research only high-fit investors; do not mass-message. | Continue only if at least two conversations identify a recurring workflow problem. |
| Days 15–21 | Offer a read-only Risk Map to the strongest fit. | Build the single feature most often requested by qualified prospects. | Prepare an investor proof packet: problem, demo, learning, asks. | Offer paid monitoring only after a useful Risk Map review. |
| Days 22–30 | Convert qualified pilots to a monthly monitoring offer. | Publish a permissioned or anonymized evidence walkthrough. | Submit the YC application if founder-approved; request warm introductions to specialist investors where available. | Decide whether the current wedge has repeatable demand or needs repositioning. |

## External Action Controls

1. Do not send a prospect message, investor email, application, forum post, or social post without the founder’s specific approval immediately before that action.
2. Do not state that a company is a customer, design partner, or user unless it has explicitly agreed.
3. Do not claim prevented incidents, quantified savings, or revenue until the evidence exists.
4. For community routes, contribute only where the channel rules permit it and never cross-post the same pitch.
5. Record every conversation outcome in a founder-controlled tracker: contacted, replied, discovery, Risk Map, pilot, paid, declined, or unqualified.
