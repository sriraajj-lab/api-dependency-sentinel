# Workprint Novelty Scan

## Exact product-match test

Workprint would have a direct product match if a tool already offers all of the following as one coherent product: it accepts company operational evidence and metrics; discovers current workflows and bottlenecks; relates the diagnosis to business KPIs; proposes or prioritizes automation/agent interventions; supports controlled deployment; and monitors impact versus a baseline.

## Direct match: Salesforce Process Intelligence + Agentforce

**Source:** [Salesforce, “What Is Process Intelligence?”](https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/operations/process-intelligence/), published 3 June 2026.

Salesforce describes process intelligence as collecting system and task data, modeling a living digital twin of workflows, dependencies, and bottlenecks, then using AI to analyze and predict business processes. Its cited capabilities include automatic workflow discovery, bottleneck and deviation detection, root-cause analysis, real-time monitoring, forecasting, and simulation. It explicitly says that process intelligence identifies automation-ready workflows and that improvements can be implemented by AI agents, including agents coordinating cross-functional work.

**Assessment:** This is a substantial direct match to the broad Workprint vision. A generic product positioned as “ingest company data, diagnose process gaps, recommend agents, and improve performance” would compete with a major incumbent category and would not be novel enough to pursue as stated. The remaining opening, if any, is not the broad mechanism; it is a very specific underserved market, data pack, metric, and preconfigured metric-to-agent loop that this kind of enterprise platform does not serve economically.

## Direct match: KYP.ai Agentic Process Intelligence

**Source:** [KYP.ai, “Best Automated Process Discovery Tools for 2026 Compared”](https://kyp.ai/automated-process-discovery-tools/), 12 August 2026.

KYP.ai describes an agentic process-intelligence platform that captures activity across systems and user desktops, discovers actual end-to-end work, quantifies and ranks automation candidates by ROI, and turns observed work into production-ready agent code. It presents a transformation engine that attaches a business case to opportunities and an agentic AI enabler that can generate platform-agnostic agent code for deployment in UiPath, SAP Joule, Microsoft Copilot Studio, or another agent environment. It also describes a conversational interface for ROI-ranked answers.

**Assessment:** KYP.ai is an even closer direct match. It combines the core Workprint claims of messy-work observation, bottleneck discovery, ROI prioritisation, and agent generation. Its enterprise implementation and data-collection model may be too heavy for small agencies, but the broad “operations cartographer that creates agents” thesis is not novel. This materially strengthens a recommendation to **stop pursuing Workprint as a horizontal platform**.

## Preliminary whitespace

There may still be room for a radically simpler, vertical diagnostic product that consumes only a small export pack rather than desktop telemetry, focuses on one metric, and provides one prebuilt intervention rather than general agent code. That is a distinct company from the original Workprint vision and needs separate competitive validation.

## Adjacent category: UiPath Agentic Automation

**Source:** [UiPath Agentic Automation](https://www.uipath.com/platform/agentic-automation), accessed 17 August 2026.

The UiPath page could not be extracted beyond its page title from the available session, so it is included only as a category reference. UiPath is already represented in KYP.ai’s comparison as a major RPA-led discovery and agent-deployment ecosystem. No detailed capability claim from the inaccessible page is used in this assessment.

**Assessment:** This does not change the conclusion. The direct matches above are sufficient to rule out the horizontal Workprint thesis; UiPath reinforces that agentic automation and workflow mining are mature competitive categories.

## Direct match: Skan AI Process Agents

**Source:** [Skan AI, “Agentic AI ROI: The Process Intelligence Foundation”](https://www.skan.ai/blogs/agentic-ai-enterprise-process-intelligence-roi), 22 May 2026.

Skan describes a process-intelligence foundation built from observed real desktop workflows, including clicks, keystrokes, decisions, workarounds, and cross-application handoffs. It says this creates a digital twin of operations and positions its Large Action Models as learning from an organisation’s actual workflow sequences rather than general text. The product framing connects that operational model to process agents that act across end-to-end workflows, escalate genuine exceptions, and improve from observed operational data.

**Assessment:** Skan independently matches the crucial Workprint premise: do not force users to manually describe ideal workflows; observe how work is really done, derive operational context, and use it to create agents that improve outcomes. Although its customer segment is enterprise, the conceptual overlap is direct. The broad Workprint vision is therefore already an active and well-funded market category—not an unoccupied innovation space.

## Directional conclusion before final recommendation

Three independent competitor paths now cover the core broad idea:

| Product | Observes or models current work | Finds bottlenecks / ties to business value | Recommends or creates agentic action | Segment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Salesforce Process Intelligence + Agentforce | Yes | Yes | Yes | Enterprise CRM / operations |
| KYP.ai Agentic Process Intelligence | Yes, including desktop activity | Yes, ROI-ranked | Yes, production-ready agent code | Enterprise / BPO / shared services |
| Skan AI | Yes, including desktop activity | Yes, process and outcome context | Yes, process agents | Enterprise operations |

The user should not pursue Workprint as a company-wide operating-system or “agents build themselves from messy company data” startup. A potentially viable derivative must be framed as a **much narrower vertical product**, with a simpler input model and one pre-built intervention. That derivative should be tested as a separate idea, not defended as novelty in the broad Workprint category.
